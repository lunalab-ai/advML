## Research question

When can local inference look numerically stable while answering the wrong probabilistic question, and how can a structural intervention in the computation reveal or correct the error?

You will investigate binary pairwise models with spins $s_i\in\{-1,+1\}$:

$$
p(s)=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\left(\sum_i h_i s_i+\sum_{(i,j)\in E}J_{ij}s_i s_j\right).
$$

Code states `0` and `1` represent spins `-1` and `+1`, respectively. A positive coupling favors agreement; a negative coupling favors disagreement. Couplings are log-potential parameters, not transition probabilities. Parameters are fixed for inference; you are not asked to fit a model to real data.

The provided module contains a small exact enumeration oracle, tree sum-product and a synchronous loopy-BP baseline. You may use these baselines. Merely wrapping them and plotting their outputs does not complete this project. The new exact cycle method and your proposed extension must be your implementation, supported by your own derivation and tests. Do not use a library's general-purpose exact inference solver for those assessed components.

## B. Derive and implement an exact cycle correction

Choose a single-cycle model and one cycle node $C$. Conditioning on $C=c$ and absorbing its incident factors into its neighbors leaves a chain on the other nodes. Derive a method that uses tree inference on the reduced graph and reconstructs the original one-node marginals.

Your derivation must identify:

- the reduced factors, including which terms become constants;
- what each reduced partition function represents;
- the correct relative weight of each value of $C$;
- how to recover the marginal of $C$ and of every remaining node;
- the difference between an unnormalized evidence mass and a probability;
- the computational and memory costs in terms of node count and state count.

Implement `cycle_cutset_exact(model, cut_node)` returning at least `marginals` and `log_z`. Do not call enumeration inside it. Validate against enumeration on at least ten small models, including unequal node fields, mixed-sign couplings and a nonsymmetric pairwise table. Agreement in both marginals and log normalizer should be within $10^{-8}$ in float64. State if an extreme test needs a justified relaxed tolerance.

Also test that multiplying any factor by a positive constant leaves normalized marginals unchanged while changing the log normalizer by the known amount. An algorithm that averages normalized conditional answers with equal weights will often look plausible on symmetric examples; your tests must detect that failure. The provided `condition` method clamps values but deliberately retains graph edges; you must perform the actual reduction, not feed the unchanged cyclic graph into the tree solver.

Demonstrate your method on a 60-node cycle where enumeration is intentionally unavailable. This is a scalability demonstration, not a claim that you have obtained an independent exact oracle at that size. Validate using structural identities, alternative cut-node choices and numerical invariances. All messages and evidence weights must remain numerically stable.
